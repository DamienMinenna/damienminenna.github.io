About

I work on superconducting magnets for particle accelerators and MRI at Irfu, the CEA institute for fundamental research, in its Department of Accelerators, Cryogenics and Magnetism (DACM).

My work sits between physics and computing. I lead the development of ALESIA, a platform that optimises magnet designs across electromagnetism, mechanics and quench behaviour, and I am the AI expert of my laboratory.

Before that, I spent several years on wave–particle interaction: traveling-wave tubes for satellite telecommunications with Thales and the French space agency (CNES), then electromagnetic pulses in laser–plasma experiments. I hold a PhD in physics.

Projects

ALESIA

An optimisation and data-management platform for superconducting magnet design. It combines nonlinear programming, evolutionary algorithms, active learning and surrogate models, and drives physics codes such as OPERA and CAST3M so that magnetic, conductor, mechanical and quench constraints are optimised together.

Preprint, arXiv 2026 Poster, MT29, Boston 2025

DIMOHA

A time-domain code for traveling-wave tubes, the microwave amplifiers on board telecommunication satellites. It rests on an N-body Hamiltonian model of the wave–particle interaction and runs in parallel on HPC clusters. Developed with Thales, CNES and the PIIM laboratory (Aix-Marseille University, CNRS).

Fortran, MPI, Python
